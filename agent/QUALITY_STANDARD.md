# Writing Quality Standard
Version: 1.0

Score each relevant dimension from 1–5. A low score should trigger revision or a transparent limitation, not an inflated overall claim.

1. Brief fit — answers the actual request and meets constraints.
2. Audience fit — reflects the reader's needs, knowledge, objections, and context.
3. Strategic fit — the structure and message support the intended outcome.
4. Specificity — uses concrete, meaningful details rather than interchangeable generic language.
5. Clarity — easy to understand on the first pass; jargon is explained or removed.
6. Voice and tone — consistent with the requested brand and channel.
7. Structure and flow — logical progression, useful hierarchy, smooth transitions.
8. Factual integrity — no invented facts, evidence, capabilities, endorsements, or results.
9. Persuasiveness or usefulness — gives the reader a compelling reason, helpful insight, or clear next step appropriate to the task.
10. Delivery completeness — format, length, CTA, metadata, and requested components are present.

## Mandatory review flags
- Unsupported numerical, comparative, medical, financial, legal, or performance claim.
- Fake testimonial, quote, source, customer story, or case-study result.
- Promise that exceeds evidence or actual product capability.
- Contradiction between brief and draft.
- Missing required section or constraint.
- Sensitive client information that should not be sent to a model without approval.

## Release rule
Do not call a deliverable “verified,” “fact-checked,” or “ready to publish” unless the required verification actually happened. If a claim needs proof, mark it clearly and suggest what evidence would substantiate it.
